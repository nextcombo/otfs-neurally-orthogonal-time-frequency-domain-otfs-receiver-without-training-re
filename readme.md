# [OTFS] Neurally Orthogonal Time-Frequency Domain (OTFS) Receiver without Training Requirements - [Included MATLAB Code]

Abstract Orthogonal Time-Frequency Spacing (OTFS) modulation technology is a highly promising 6G air interface technology for high-speed mobile communication. In recent years, OTFS receivers have relied on deep neural networks (DNNs) to mitigate air interface interference, but such solutions require large-scale, environment-specific datasets for training before deployment. This paper proposes an OTFS receiver that employs Joint Channel Estimation and Symbol Detection (JCED), eliminating the need for datasets and training processes, dubbed Untrained Neural Network (UNN)-JCED. Simulation results show that UNN-JCED outperforms existing OTFS receivers in terms of normalized mean square error (NMSE) for channel estimation and symbol error rate (SER) for detection by more than 1dB.
Introduction Based on the existing 5G air interface receivers using Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), these receivers perform well in frequency selective fading channels due to multipath delay spread. However, when objects such as transmitters, receivers, or reflectors are moving at high speeds, Doppler shift causes time-varying channel path delays, forming a dual selective fading channel, which leads to performance degradation of the receiver. Literature [1] proposed the Orthogonal Time-Frequency Spacing (OTFS) modulation technique, which allows receivers to identify the time delay and Doppler shift of each channel path, making it a highly promising candidate technology for 6G high-speed mobile communication air interfaces.
In recent years, research on OTFS receivers has utilized deep neural networks (DNNs) to simplify symbol detection design. However, these receivers require extensive optimization of neural network parameters before deployment, which relies on massive datasets. The Bayesian Parallel Interference Cancellation Network (BPICNet) proposed in [4] reduces the number of neural network parameters to support high-speed mobile vehicle communication. Each layer of the neural network uses only three parameters, and its symbol error rate (SER) performance is close to the near-optimal expected-to-go (EP), with lower complexity but still requiring a massive training dataset. To further reduce complexity, [6] proposed message passing-based symbol detection and threshold-based channel estimation methods. These OTFS receivers' channel estimation processes are conducted separately from jointly optimizing channel estimation and symbol detection (JCED). Compared to JCED with joint optimization, their performance is suboptimal. Previous studies have explored JCED using both training-based deep neural networks [7] and Bayesian approaches [8,9], with applications in vehicular networking, low Earth orbit satellites, and underwater communications. Training-based deep neural network JCED [7] requires large-scale training datasets and is difficult to adapt to different channel conditions during training compared to those at test time. When the channel conditions between training and testing differ, the SER performance of such receivers can drop by more than 1dB [10]. Bayesian JCED [8,9] does not require training and does not face generalization issues, but involves iterative matrix inversion, which has higher complexity. These limitations have spurred research into new JCED methods that do not require training datasets, can achieve generalization, and have low complexity.
Recently, [11] proposed an untrained neural network (UNN) for channel estimation without training datasets and matrix inversion. This approach is similar to that of [12], which utilizes convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to recover clean signals directly from noisy observations. The performance of the UNN in [11,12] depends on both channel coefficients and image correlation features, limiting its effectiveness in OTFS receiver design since the transmitted OTFS symbols are generally uncorrelated with channel coefficients. To address this issue, in earlier research [13,14], a fully connected neural network (FCNN)-based UNN was proposed to support high-speed mobile vehicle communication, achieving symbol detection without the need for inter-symbol correlation. Specifically, by integrating the FCNN-based UNN with the BPIC scheme [15], the UNN-BPIC achieved comparable SER performance to near-optimal expected-to-be-optimized (EP) [5] and outperformed BPICNet [4], without the use of training datasets or matrix inversion. In [13,14], the neural network parameters were optimized for each received signal, avoiding generalization issues across different channel conditions. However, existing methods based on untrained neural networks [13,14] did not leverage data symbol optimization for channel estimation as JCED does, thus leaving a research gap in extending untrained neural networks to JCED.
To fill this research gap, this paper proposes a novel OTFS receiver (UNN-JCED) for joint channel estimation and symbol detection in high-speed moving vehicle communication based on untrained neural networks. The main contribution of this paper is the introduction of the first neural OTFS receiver that does not require training datasets or matrix inversion for JCED. Simulation results show that the UNN-JCED outperforms existing OTFS receivers by more than 1dB in terms of normalized mean square error (NMSE) for channel estimation and symbol error rate (SER) for detection.
Conclusion: This paper introduces a neural orthogonal time-frequency space (OTFS) receiver for joint channel estimation and symbol detection (JCED). Simulation results indicate that the UNN-JCED outperforms current state-of-the-art OTFS receivers by more than 1dB in terms of normalized mean square error (NMSE) for channel estimation and symbol error rate (SER) for detection. Future research will extend this method to fractional Doppler scenarios to better align with real-world communication environments.
